# Learning design

Audience: interested adult beginners who want to interpret human behaviour using evidence. The gap is between attaching an intuitive label and explaining a case with accurate concepts, alternatives and limits. Delivery is a standalone, accessible, learner-paced GitHub Pages course, not an LMS or certification.

The established café visual identity uses Libre Baskerville headings, Arimo body text, pale blue backgrounds, navy text, teal interactions and restrained chapter accents. Reading and controls precede the supporting figure in the stacked layout. The primary learning path remains clear when research disclosures stay closed.

Each chapter has a first decision, three separate narrated explanations, a visible evidence check, a new written transfer case, unaided retrieval and low-stakes field practice. The final case integrates chapters. Page visits indicate navigation, not mastery. No learner pilot or independent academic review has been conducted.

## 1. Different ages, different questions

Objective: Distinguish an age difference from evidence of developmental change and propose a better comparison.

Initial decision: What would best improve Leo’s age claim?

Transfer: A library compares current twenty-year-olds and eighty-year-olds on a digital search task. Their schooling and computer experience differ. Which limitation matters in this study?

Field application: Compare two non-sensitive learning tasks in your own experience. Name the outcome and two influences other than age. Do not assess another person’s abilities.

## 2. An early start is not a fixed script

Objective: Explain how timing, dose and context qualify a prenatal risk claim.

Initial decision: How should the poster’s claim be revised?

Transfer: A friend is concerned about a past exposure during pregnancy after reading a general risk headline. What is the most appropriate response?

Field application: Choose a public developmental-risk headline. Identify its exposure, outcome and uncertainty without investigating anyone’s private history.

## 3. Small actions, larger questions

Objective: Distinguish infant task performance from an unrestricted claim about what an infant knows.

Initial decision: What does the longer look establish most directly?

Transfer: An infant looks toward a hidden toy but does not reach to retrieve it in a more demanding search task. What should the researcher consider first?

Field application: Read a public infant-study summary. Separate the measured action from the claimed mental ability. Do not test or record a child.

## 4. Learning with a little help

Objective: Design support that distinguishes independent performance from performance with assistance.

Initial decision: What would show independent learning more clearly?

Transfer: A bilingual child uses few English words during a brief workshop but communicates fluently with a family member in another language. Which response best fits the evidence?

Field application: For your own learning, choose a task you can do with a guide. Reduce one prompt, then try a new example and record what still needs support.

## 5. Security is a relationship question

Objective: Explain attachment as a context-sensitive relationship pattern without assigning a fixed identity.

Initial decision: What can Leo reasonably conclude?

Transfer: A child startles easily but seeks comfort from a familiar caregiver and later resumes play. Which interpretation is best supported?

Field application: Use a fictional film scene to distinguish a description of support-seeking from an attachment label. Do not classify real children or partners.

## 6. The rules around the child

Objective: Identify interacting contexts that could contribute to a developmental observation.

Initial decision: What should Sam examine first?

Transfer: A study finds that children whose caregivers attend school events have higher average attainment, but work schedules and resources were not measured. What is a plausible alternative explanation?

Field application: Map the contexts in a fictional learner’s participation problem. Include one indirect influence and one practical environmental change.

## 7. Adolescence is more than a warning label

Objective: Explain adolescent decisions using context and development without treating age as incapacity.

Initial decision: Which explanation fits Jo’s contrasting performance best?

Transfer: A youth workshop includes an unfamiliar procedure with real safety requirements. What should the team do?

Field application: Review a public headline about adolescents. Identify its measure and whether the conclusion exceeds the sample or task.

## 8. Adult lives take different routes

Objective: Distinguish a socially patterned transition from a universal developmental requirement.

Initial decision: What is wrong with Leo’s schedule claim?

Transfer: A volunteer wants to keep cataloguing books but finds small print difficult. Which example best illustrates compensation?

Field application: Choose a change in your own low-stakes routine. Identify one goal, one resource and one environmental constraint without ranking your life against a timetable.

## 9. Ageing includes different kinds of change

Objective: Differentiate normal variation, task-specific age trends and concerns requiring professional assessment.

Initial decision: What does Pat’s menu difficulty show?

Transfer: A person reports a persistent new decline affecting ordinary daily activities, beyond a single unfamiliar task. Which response is appropriate?

Field application: Inspect a public interface for avoidable visual or memory demands. Suggest an accessibility change without using an age stereotype.

## 10. A developmental explanation should open options

Objective: Build a developmental account that links time, context, evidence and proportionate support.

Initial decision: Which workshop plan best follows the course?

Transfer: Participants omit fewer steps with a checklist during one workshop; no later unassisted task is measured. What conclusion is justified?

Field application: Write a short recommendation for a fictional mixed-age workshop: outcome, support, comparison, follow-up and one limitation.
